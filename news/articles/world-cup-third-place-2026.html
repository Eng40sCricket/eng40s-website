England Over 40s Cricket — 7 November 2026

England Over 40s have returned from Guyana with third place at the IMC Over 40s World Cup, winning six of their eight matches and falling just two wickets short of a place in the Grand Final.

The campaign included a seven-wicket group win over Pakistan, the eventual world champions, and finished with a record 431/3 against South Africa in the third-place play-off, built on a world-record unbeaten 231 from captain Darren Stevens.

Group stage: five wins from six

England opened with emphatic wins over the USA (by 134 runs) and New Zealand (by 129 runs), before Iresh Saxena's 4/5 bowled Scotland out for 106 in Berbice. At Everest CC, Steve Naylor (4/29) and an unbeaten 119 from Ben Frazer secured victory over Pakistan. The only group defeat came against India, by 25 runs, and the response was immediate: Taqi Abbas took 7/25, the bowling performance of the tour, as England beat the Rest of the Caribbean to reach the Cup semi-finals.

Semi-final: two wickets from the final

A rematch with Pakistan at the Guyana National Stadium, Providence, proved the tightest game of the tournament. From 104/5, Ryan Canning's composed unbeaten 53 lifted England to 205/9. Frazer (2/26) and Jonny Wightman (2/30) took the game deep, but Pakistan got home by two wickets and went on to beat Australia in the Grand Final.

A world record to finish

Against South Africa in the third-place play-off, England recovered from 10/2 to post 431/3. Stevens made 231 not out from 144 balls, with 19 fours and 14 sixes, the highest individual score in IMC cricket in any age group, beating the 195 Abdul Razzaq made for Pakistan against Australia at the 2023 Global Cup in Karachi. Canning added an unbeaten 100 from 70 balls in an unbroken stand of 271, and Saxena (5/14) and Abbas (3/17) bowled South Africa out for 153 to win by 278 runs.

Tour leaders

  • Most runs: Darren Stevens, 421 (including 22 sixes)
  • Most wickets: Iresh Saxena, 15 at 10.47
  • Best bowling: Taqi Abbas, 7/25 v Rest of the Caribbean
  • Wicketkeeping: Ryan Canning, 10 dismissals (8 catches, 2 stumpings)

All 16 tourists played their part. Our thanks go to head coach David Hughes, scorer Alan Jones, commentator Tony Wood, soft tissue specialist James Wright, our travelling supporters and sponsors, and our hosts at the Cricket West Indies Masters Association for a superbly staged tournament.
